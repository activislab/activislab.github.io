Each target starts with the paper’s Figure 6 power-law amplitude fit and a uniform direction, then is weighted by Itti saliency and short-lived inhibition of return. This is a 2D radial adaptation of ρ(x|y) ∝ s(x)f(|x−y|); finite-field rejection and IOR are explicit additions to Brockmann and Geisel’s one-dimensional model. The empirical fit is used because their theoretical Cauchy scale was not fitted in visual degrees.

Simulator constants, not Brockmann–Geisel estimates: up to 256 valid target proposals, saliency floor 0.045, IOR floor 0.03, 3.2° IOR width, 700 ms decay, and 3.2 s retention.

The 42-map feature architecture follows Itti et al.; orientation filtering uses a phase-invariant 9 × 9 quadrature adaptation (period 7 px, σ = 7/3) because the 1998 paper does not fix those kernel details.

04 · Display & field

Visual field extent: 220° horizontal × 133° vertical. Eccentricity is measured from fixation in this angular coordinate system.

Simulated eye movements

Five-minute scanpath distributions

Each saccade duration is sampled around its amplitude-dependent mean (15% coefficient of variation; simulator assumption), then peak velocity is derived from the minimum-jerk trajectory. Intersaccadic intervals are measured from one saccade’s offset to the next onset.

Pause the action potential, move the electrode, and compare the traces. The two recordings use different voltage scales: millivolts inside the cell and microvolts outside.

01 · Follow the membrane voltage

The intracellular trace shows the prescribed somatic action potential. Moving the extracellular electrode leaves this trace unchanged.

02 · Explore the extracellular field

The extracellular trace depends on the electrode’s location relative to current sources and sinks. Try positions near the soma, along the dendrites, and farther away; compare both the waveform and its amplitude.

03 · Inspect a moment in time

Drag the timeline to pause at any point in the 5 ms cycle. Blue and red indicate negative and positive extracellular potential. Contours connect equal potentials; the optional ion arrows illustrate somatic Na⁺ entry and K⁺ exit.

Illustrative model · assumptions & interpretation

Prescribed action-potential waveform and six balanced transmembrane current compartments (ΣI = 0), not a conductance-based neuron. Extracellular potential is calculated by superposition: φₑ = Σ Iⱼ / (4πσrⱼ), with σ = 0.3 S/m. With current in nA and distance in µm, multiplication by 1000 gives µV. Dendritic currents are distributed uniformly along short segments using numerical line-source integration. A finite source-radius cutoff avoids singularities. The conductor is homogeneous, isotropic and infinite in three dimensions; the display shows a two-dimensional slice, with reference at infinity. Positive transmembrane current is outward (source); negative current is inward (sink). Current locations and passive return currents are schematic. The detailed pyramidal-cell drawing is an anatomical illustration; its fine branches do not add current compartments to the original six-compartment model. The axon is drawn for orientation, not as a simulated propagating action potential. Membrane voltage and currents are prescribed independently and are not solved self-consistently. A remote-reference intracellular measurement approximates Vₘ when the local extracellular contribution is small. Amplifier ground symbols denote the recording reference. Ion arrows illustrate somatic Na⁺ entry and K⁺ exit; they are not simulated individual ion trajectories or the complete membrane current. The extracellular trace represents a single-cell spike-related potential, not a population LFP. Volume-conductor model reference: LFPykit.
